is

Atman is Brahman. The word “is” sits on the crown of the tree, and an arrow falls from it into the word YOGA lettered in the canopy — yuj, to yoke, to harness. Both are drawn faintly, on purpose: they are there if you hold your attention on them, which is itself the sixth limb, dharana. The whole page is a yoga lesson.

ATMAN IS BRAHMAN

yuj — to yoke, to harness. The middle word is the practice.

The whole load is carried by the smallest word, and the smallest word is the same root as the name of the thing you came here to do.

So it is not a description of a state you arrive at later, after enough mornings on the mat. The yoking is not the reward for the sentence. The yoking is the sentence.

Which leaves an obvious problem, and it is worth saying out loud before anybody sits down: in here, out there, us and other — a necessary illusion to order coffee.

Because if it really is all one, walls are hard to avoid, and the car still has to be driven somewhere. “I want” — what does that even mean? The Buddha said I should have no wants. And yet somebody has to decide about dinner.

So the split gets called an illusion and then kept anyway, because it is load-bearing. That is not hypocrisy. That is the practice. You see through it and you order the coffee.

True enlightenment is useless. Spend too long in nirvana and you will wind up sitting in a pile of crap.

Which is the entire method, stated as plainly as it can be stated: find the seat. Turn the brain off. Unplug it — and then plug it back in.

Find sukhasana. Find samadhi. And leave before you need to change your shorts.
