## 1 · History of Meditation — the chronology

His spine, in his order:

- **Before everything.** The universe may have begun as a meditation: Logos, the word of God,
  Vishnu's dream, the sound of om. *"So meditation may be as old as the Universe itself."*
- Some theories: **chanting and toning predate language and are what led to language.** Wilder
  ones: meditation is what evolved us into this species.
- **~3000 BCE** — scholars back-estimate the practice in India from the texts.
- **1500 BCE** — the Vedas describe meditation as *already ancient and formal*.
- **~1000 BCE** — Torah refers to meditative practice as common, well-understood behaviour.
- **~600 BCE** — Taoism. Less spiritual, more systematic: meditation as a **skill or tool** for
  improving oneself. First real move toward benefits beyond the spiritual.
- **Same window** — Buddhism in India. The entire practice can be considered meditation.
- **The Silk Road** spreads it worldwide — *"most likely the origin of meditation as we know it."*
- The tell that it is one lineage and not parallel invention: **Buddhists use the same word the
  Vedas and Yoga use — dhyana.** Siddhartha was Indian; Vedanta was the water he swam in.
- **Confucianism** arrives as an overview of the "100 schools of thought."
- **Zen / Chinese Buddhism** — systematic, almost scientific; possibly an attempt to make sense of
  the flood of practices as they spread.
- A couple of centuries on: the Mediterranean. **Greeks value it above all other subjects**; it
  enters Judaism, Sufism, early Christianity in the first centuries CE.
- **6th c. CE** — reaches Japan. By then the whole modern world knows about it.
- **~600 quiet years.** Monks develop and pass it on without much changing.

### ⭐ THE 11th CENTURY — his best observation in the doc

*"Around the 11th century we see meditation get a makeover across the board."* Four things at once,
and he flags that experts still argue whether they influenced each other:

- Sufi practice **formalized and systematized**
- **Repetition of prayers in specific postures** across the Byzantine empire
- A Latin monk writes **"read, ponder, pray, contemplate"** into a formal structure he calls a ladder
- **Zen begins being taught separately from Buddhism**

> **⚑ anchor — this convergence is real and every piece has a name.** Worth having in his pocket,
> because it turns a nice observation into a citable one:
> - the ladder is **Guigo II**, a Carthusian, *Scala Claustralium*, c. 1150 — *lectio, meditatio,
>   oratio, contemplatio*, which is his four words exactly
> - the Byzantine practice is **hesychasm** / the Jesus Prayer — Symeon the New Theologian d. 1022
> - Sufi **dhikr** formalizes in the same window — al-Ghazali d. 1111
> - **Chan** gets its Song-dynasty codification in the same window
>
> So it is a **second convergence**, ~1700 years after the first one. If the Axial Age is the
> setup, this is the rhyme — and this time there *were* trade roads, which makes the "independent
> or not" question sharper rather than settled.

- Another 600 years → the **Enlightenment**. The West rediscovers it. **Hatha practiced for the
  first time in centuries in British India.** The Gita translated into many languages. Whitman,
  Thoreau, *Walden*.
- **1927** — Tibetan Book of the Dead translated. *(⚑ Evans-Wentz, correct.)*
- Secularized variants "for use by non-Hindus" — **TM in the 1960s**; Ashtanga Vinyasa becomes
  simply "Yoga" in western terminology.
- **1931** — first scientific study of meditation. *(⚑ about right; the well-documented early wave
  is Brosse's India EEG expedition 1935, then Das & Gastaut 1955.)*
- Peak **late 70s–early 80s**, then quiet until MRI and CT make it cheap again. *"This class being
  another example of that wave."*
- **2001** — *"When I first took Yoga in college it was called 'stretch and relaxation' in the
  course catalog."* ⭐ **The best pin on the whole timeline.** It is inside his own lifetime, it
  is a primary source, and it dates the exact moment the west was still embarrassed by the word.

## 2 · A Meditation on Meditation — three claims

Structured as **Nothing → Something → Anything**, and the structure is the argument.

**Opening bit** — the script of his one-man show: `"?"` *[asks questioningly]* · `"."` *[responds
firmly]*. Then Roshi's *"just sit zazen, that's it"* and Suzuki's *"Zen Buddhism, very hard to
understand, thank you."*

### Meditation is Nothing
- The space between thoughts where **duality disappears**. On the cushion the eyes are superfluous,
  I-statements don't apply, *"and it feels great."*
- **The garlic test.** You can cite brain scans and neurochemistry and it still is not the taste of
  garlic. If you know, you know. If you don't — try it, it's good for you.
- **Doing nothing is hard.** There is a reason Angry Birds and Netflix are popular; solitary
  confinement is cruel and unusual punishment. *"We will do just about anything to not be alone
  with our thoughts."*
- The render: photon → cone → dendrite → visual cortex → language centre. *"Does it sound like me
  or does it sound like you?"*
- On top of the simulacrum sits **a narrator** — a secondary layer of opinion, littered with
  attachments and aversions, mostly dopamine-scored for survivability.
- Buddha: **dukkha ~ bad empty**, **sukkha ~ good empty**. Suffering is not from perceiving the
  world but from that second layer. The path is the middle way.
- A large slice of consciousness is spent managing **facial affect** — because a few thousand years
  ago reading anger kept you alive. On the cushion you can just let the face relax.
- **The stubbed toe.** The pain is the brain programming itself not to do that again; from outside
  it looks like yelling at inanimate objects. Bumps you never noticed had no lesson to teach.
  *Except tooth pain — he names that as the one aversion he has never beaten.*
- ⚠ **The hardest and best passage, and it is autobiographical.** His first hospitalization was for
  **catatonia** — completely unresponsive, nothing distracting him from his meditation. He names
  being **addicted to doing nothing**, and says it is what led to his chronic homelessness.
  *"If you sit in Zazen long enough you eventually wind up in a pile of crap."*
  → This is the origin of the samadhi line already on the site. On the page it reads as a joke.
  In the doc it is a warning he earned. **His call how much of that goes public.**

### Meditation is Something
- **The white bear** (Dostoevsky) → give the mind **a brown bear first** → that is **dharana**.
- **Executive function is the ability to inhibit stimuli**, and choosing what to attend to is the
  only thing we actually control. *"Free will is a delusion of the hallucination of our sense
  organs."* Attention *"is quickly becoming currency."*
- **Asana** — the proprioceptive map. The pinky toe you weren't thinking about until now.
- **Pranayama** — breath is the rare thing that is both automatic and controllable, which is why
  every book on mastering anything ends up at it. X in X out → add pauses → find the heartbeat in
  the pause → all three parts of the lungs → tidal motion up and down the spine → then keep it
  without thinking about it, which takes years.
- ⭐ **The ice bucket study** — flagged in the doc as *possibly invented by his teacher; he could
  never find it*. Three groups held on longer than the rational baseline:
  1. **ignore it** — turn the volume down. Works briefly.
  2. **transmute it** — decide it feels good. Works longer, then fails.
  3. **focus on it** — no ignoring, no reframing, just attend to the sensation. **Lasted longest,
     some had to be told to stop.** No suffering, just sensation. *"You really can't tell if it is
     cold or hot."*
  → This is the whole basis of his pratyahara: **not withdrawal, but diving in so deeply you can
  no longer distinguish down.**
- **Sound.** Two ear signals → a 3D soundscape. *"They say when the 2 ears become one enlightenment
  has been reached."* The garbage truck solo. *"My suffering was coming from being annoyed at being
  annoyed. I was distracted by being annoyed at being distracted."*
- **99 senses**, or choose one.

### Meditation is Anything
- Om Mani Padme Hum. *"Buddhists are awfully attached to non-attachment. What's with the aversion
  to aversions? Seeking enlightenment is a bit of an oxymoron."*
- **Guided visualization is the exact opposite of present-moment mindfulness — and both are
  meditation.** Which proves the point: *"what you focus on isn't nearly as important as just
  focusing. It is like exercise — how you move is not nearly as important as just moving."*
- Kabat-Zinn's **laser-like focus**. You can't focus on nothing, so focus on one thing and back
  away slowly.
- **How he actually teaches it:** remind them they have a body in space, that they control it with
  their mind — *telekinesis* — that it is breathing, and that everything past that is a choice.

## 3 · A Brief History of Nothing

- Big bang vs *"fast expansion period"* — splitting hairs, and *"we're just arguing semantics"* is
  itself a manipulative way to end an argument.
- The **Brahma Bull** pronounces the sacred syllable, beginning **Vishnu's Dream** — a metaphor for
  the universe holding itself to a single thread of the multiverse **through observation**.
- The fractal: impersonal Brahman → Atman → a crude simulacrum inside each of us → the hologram
  repeats.
- ⭐⭐ **THE PROOF, and it is the origin of the coffee line already on the site:**
  *"You wanna know how I know it is a copy? I am always at the geographical and geometric centre of
  it. This egocentric view of the universe is handy for ordering a cup of coffee but it makes it
  obvious I am hallucinating this whole thing."*
- *"Namacray. The crazy in me honors and acknowledges the crazy in you."*
- Arjuna remembering every lifetime = **zooming out one level of the fractal.** At that moment you
  are basically Vishnu. Vishnu cannot enter the dream directly — hence avatars.
- *"I am God, but you are too."* · *"I can assure you I am not The One. You are."*
- ⭐ *"Instead of what would Jesus do — **what would you do to Jesus?** Now treat every sentient
  creature that way. Including you."* Raised Catholic; the idea that any sentient being might be
  the hand of God appealed to him.
- The bit about Christ/Krishna making Hindus impossible to convert — *"oh yeah, I know this story"* —
  is the funniest thing in any of the three docs and it makes the syncretism argument in one beat.
- **Cycles.** 10,000 years ago sucked. 15,000 years ago a human survived a broken femur — *"no way
  they pulled that off alone."* Comet, ice age, volcano — all paths converge on a flood. All that
  survives is weird rock piles and graffiti. Sumerians and Egyptians *"held the best raves."*
  Gilgamesh blurs art, philosophy and religion → Greek comedy → passion plays → the MCU.
  Then 5,000 years ago everything sucked again. The Vedas say this is the fifth civilization;
  *"the first two didn't even leave behind litter."*
  > **⚑ anchor, and this one is a correction he'll want before class.** The healed-femur story is
  > almost always credited to Margaret Mead, and **there is no primary source for it** — no lecture,
  > no paper, no interview. It is a genuinely unattributable anecdote. The *point* stands on its
  > own (a healed femur means somebody carried you), so it is safer told as folklore than as Mead.
- **Hindu Kush valley: the weather was better, so they entered their anthropocene later.**
  *"Someone decided to start writing down their favourite episodes at the time, like fan fiction,
  and the Rig Vedas, Vedas and Upanishads were created."*
  > **⚑ anchor — the survival mechanism was the opposite of weather.** India's climate destroys palm
  > leaf; Egypt's dryness is why papyrus lasted. Sanskrit survived because it was stored **in
  > people** — obsessive metrical redundancy, generations of recitation. Which sets up his own
  > better line: **memorised stories stayed plastic; writing hardcoded them.** And the tradition
  > drew that line itself — **śruti** (heard, fixed) vs **smṛti** (remembered, revisable).
- **The Shiva / Parvati / Ganesh story**, told in full and at length — Shiva gone for decades, the
  discus with a self-cleaning cycle, the beheading, the search for a spare head, the enlightened
  elephant on his way to the graveyard who volunteers his own. Ends on Ganesh's **broom** (clutter
  and confusion), **axe** (hindrance) and **noose** (selfishness and ego).
- **Shiva is entropy.** The only constant is change; this too shall pass. Pure chaos.
  **Om Namah Shivaya: "may my karma come quickly and severely."** Nataraja will juggle fire at your
  party, or burn it down if you don't invite him.
- ⭐ *"The first yoga lesson on this planet was him teaching Parvati. We know he liked to meditate
  and would do it for long periods of time but **we still have no idea what he was actually
  doing**."* Either it was so ubiquitous no explanation was needed, or it was a guarded skill
  bordering on magic. *"No one's gonna buy the cow if you can get the milk for free."*
- His business motto: **"Free cow with purchase of milk."**
