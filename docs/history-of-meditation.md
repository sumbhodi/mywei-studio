History of Meditation

The universe itself may have begun as a meditation: Logos, the word of God, Vishnu's dream, the sound of om; many cosmogenic myths share this trait. Even quantum physics seems to point in that direction. So meditation may be as old as the Universe itself.

Some theories claim that chanting and toning predate language and are what led to the creation of language.

Even wilder theories claim meditation is what caused us to evolve into the species we are today. What we do know for sure is that meditation predates recorded history.

The oldest texts known to man are the Vedas and the Upanishads, the sacred texts of what we call Hinduism. The Vedas describe meditation as an ancient formal practice in 1,500 BCE and based on these texts scholars estimate the practice began in India in approximately 3,000 BCE or 5,000 years ago. The Torah, or old testament, comes into recorded history about 500 years later (1,000 BCE) and also refers to meditative practices as if they are common, well understood behaviors. Taoism is next chronologically and approaches the practice less spiritually and more systematically around 600 BCE. In Taoism meditation is seen more as a skill or tool that can be used to improve oneself. They begin to explore the benefits of the practice beyond its spiritual implications. Buddhism appears in India around the same time that Taoism is flourishing in China. The entire practice of Buddhism can be considered meditation. The spread of Buddhism along the silk road led to meditation being practiced throughout the world and is most likely the origin of meditation as we know it today.

Whether meditation began as a unique phenomenon across the globe or slowly spread out of the hindu kush valley is still up for debate but Buddhist use the same word, dhyana, for meditation as the Vedas and Yoga. This is most likely because the first Buddha, Siddhartha Gautama, was from India and Vedantism or Hinduism was the predominant religion in the time and place buddhism sprang from.

Either way, as Buddhism spread, so did meditation. The overlap between Taoism and Buddhism becomes difficult to distinguish and a third school of thought, Confucianism, appears at this time as an explanation or overview of the "100 schools of thought" circulating in the time period. 

Zen Buddhism or "Chinese buddhism" or tao buddhism was a systematic almost scientific approach to the art and practice that may have been an attempt to make sense of the plethora of information coming out of the varieties and practices as they spread and grew. 

A couple of centuries later the practice has spread to the mediterranean and we see the Greek's valuing the practice above all other subjects and even incorporations into judaism, sufism and early christianity in the first few centuries of our modern era. It took almost 1,000 years (6th Century ACE) for meditation to spread to Japan but by that point the entire modern world knew about meditation.

Nothing new seems to be introduced for another 600 years or so as monks around the world quietly develop and pass on their practice. Then around the 11th century we see meditation get a makeover across the board.  The practice of meditation is formalized into the sufi practice as it is systemized, the repetition of prayers in specific postures is practiced across the byzantine empire, a latin monk describes "read, ponder, pray, contemplate" into a formal structure he refers to as a ladder and Zen begins being taught separate from Buddhism all at about the same time. The experts are still debating how much these influenced each other but it is possible they all occurred relatively independently as society evolved and we had access to more information like the discovery of calculus. 

Another 600 years passes before much changes in the practice. During the "Enlightenment Period" we see a resurgence and updating of the practice as the western world rediscovers the practice. Hatha Yoga is practiced for the first time in centuries in British India. Many ancient texts like the bhagavad gita are translated and interpreted into numerous languages giving more people than ever access to the concepts. Authors such as Whitman and Thoreau give us gems like "Walden Pond". The translation of the Tibetan book of the dead was published in 1927 and "secularized variants of yoga traditions'' were developed "for use by non-Hindus”, e.g. the system of Transcendental Meditation popular in the 1960s, and numerous forms of Hatha Yoga derived from the Ashtanga Vinyasa Yoga school, which became known simply as "Yoga" in western terminology."  Even the early schools of psychology were exploring the concept. Within a generation we see meditation adopted throughout the west and in 1931 the first scientific explorations of meditation began.

This scientific exploration seems to have hit its peak around the late 70's to early 80's with not much further research available until recently. The modern availability and affordability of MRI's Ct Scans and other ways of looking at the brain have led to a resurgence of scientific academic study of meditation. This class being another example of that wave. When I first took Yoga in college in 2001 it was called "stretch and relaxation" in the course catalog. 

Today Meditation is taught as a college class, mentioned in media and pop culture, made fun of in memes etc. The concept, if not the practice, is known universally worldwide.
